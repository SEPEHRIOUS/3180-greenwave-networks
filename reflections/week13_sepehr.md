## Readings and What I Worked On

Before this week, the two papers I read most closely were Nguyen et al. (2021) and Porambage et al. (2021). I spent the most time with Nguyen et al. because it gave a broad overview of security and privacy issues across the different layers of 6G. The part that stood out most was the discussion of physical-layer risks, particularly how technologies like massive MIMO could reveal the physical location of radio equipment through signal characteristics. That showed me that privacy in 6G is not only about protecting stored or transmitted data. Porambage et al. was also useful because it connected specific 6G technologies to the security and privacy risks they could introduce. I used the other four papers more selectively to understand specific areas such as edge computing, distributed AI, federated learning, and other privacy challenges.

## Challenges

The hardest part of the Discussion was connecting the privacy literature to the idea of trust. Most of the papers describe specific technical privacy and security problems, while trust is a much broader concept. I had to avoid assuming that better privacy automatically means that users will trust a 6G system. What made the connection clearer for me was thinking of privacy and security as factors that support trust rather than guarantee it. If a network collects large amounts of data, relies heavily on AI, or exposes information in unexpected ways, users need confidence that those risks are being controlled. At the same time, technical protections by themselves are not necessarily enough to create trust.
